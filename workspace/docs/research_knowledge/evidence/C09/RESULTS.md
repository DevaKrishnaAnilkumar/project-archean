# C09 · Evolution of acquired-information retention

Stable, cheap sensing and free transfer favoured nonzero retention relative to conditional neutral turnover in the tested world. The effect was weaker when the mapping reversed or redrew independently. C09 tested selection on a supplied retention mechanism, with all agents using the same learning and sensing rules.

## Prespecified results

The independent unit is the world. The measure counts end-period T>0 occupancy over 28,800 possible population-period slots. Empty slots and extinct periods contribute zero. Passive replay preserves each C world's realised demography. Living genotype frequencies use realised living organism-periods as denominator.

| Contrast · Reward 8, cheap sensing, free transmission | Mean difference | Exploratory 95% t interval |
|---|---:|---:|
| Stable parental evolution minus passive replay | +7.14 pp | +3.25 to +11.03 |
| Stable enrichment minus periodic-reversal enrichment | +7.77 pp | +4.06 to +11.49 |
| Stable enrichment minus unpredictable enrichment | +6.98 pp | +3.13 to +10.82 |

Twenty paired seeds per contrast. Intervals are unadjusted. Stable C occupancy was 70.98% versus 63.84% passive. Nonzero retention represented 73.47% of realised living periods. T=0, 0.5 and 1 accounted for 26.53%, 32.17% and 41.30% of living periods. These are distinct from the slot-based primary measure. At the same energy settings, reversal enrichment was −0.64 pp and unpredictability +0.16 pp, with both individual intervals crossing zero.

## Secondary and control outcomes

- **Fixed full versus blank inheritance depended on the outcome.** In stable cheap free worlds, the paired first-choice success difference was +14.12 points over original birth cohorts and mean abundance +1.77. Under periodic reversal, full inheritance still improved first-choice success by +7.94 points, while average abundance fell by 7.73 organisms. These are unadjusted secondary comparisons. Population composition and donor exposure change after assignment. Higher early accuracy need not imply a demographic benefit.
- **Parent versus other surviving donor.** In stable cheap free worlds, C had mean donor Brier error 0.071 versus 0.136 in D and pooled first-choice success of 77.97% versus 75.26% among observable births. In periodic reversal the ordering of mean abundance reversed, with C at 55.84 and D at 62.51. Under independent mapping redraws, donor error was higher in C (0.372 versus 0.290). Reproductive selection filters available parents, and D samples the surviving population. The contrast does not isolate kinship. There were 18 no-alternative donor fallbacks across the grid.
- **Partial retention traded confidence against mismatch.** In parental evolving reversal worlds at reward 8 and free transfer, partial retention occupied 36.90% of living periods versus 28.37% full retention. Partial newborn belief Brier was 0.192 versus 0.208 full, and blank was 0.250. In the comparable unpredictable setting blank had the best Brier of these three (0.250 versus 0.295 and 0.365). This descriptive comparison combines selection, drift and conditional donor quality. It does not establish a universal preference for partial retention.
- **Transfer costs and information-free controls.** Adding transmission cost reduced stable free-setting enrichment from +7.14 to +6.13 points at reward 8, descriptively. For q=0.50 all beliefs stayed at 0.5. Free A/B birth timing, action and energy trajectories were byte-identical on each matched seed for the comparable fields. Costly q=0.50 fixed full transfer spent 7,701 simulated units across 20 worlds. The cost charged the recipient's retention mechanism even without useful information. Neutral-control enrichments crossed zero.
- **Extinction and scarce birth cohorts.** All 80 high-sensing-cost world-population runs went extinct. Only five seeds had births in fixed policy comparisons for that extreme condition. Treat attempt and donor quality summaries there as sparse. Across the full study 213 active worlds went extinct and 235,054 births occurred. These outcomes remain in the analysis.
- **Revision and survival.** In free reward-8 reversal C, 1,071 of 1,237 contradictory newborn beliefs eventually aligned within the initial mapping segment before censoring. Among observed alignments the mean delay was 4.27 periods after the first possible action. Age-specific death and censor counts, switch-start cohorts and survival outcomes are available separately. In unpredictable worlds a redraw itself can cause apparent alignment.

C09 identifies scoped conditions under which acquired information can be valuable. It does not demonstrate spontaneous memory architecture or evolved waiting. Replay benchmarks composition under random turnover and cannot prove population survival under a counterfactual ecology.
