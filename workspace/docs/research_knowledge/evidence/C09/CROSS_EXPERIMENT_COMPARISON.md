# Cross-experiment comparison · C09

| Existing evidence | Relationship to C09 |
|---|---|
| v1, F-001, Q-009 | C09 retains birth blocking without culling and confirms permanent extinction within the protocol. The original early v1 history remains incomplete. |
| F-007, H-009, Q-006 | Stable first choices improve under full parent transfer. Periodic reversal permits better pooled early choices alongside lower population abundance and weaker retention enrichment. Those outcomes do not contradict one-shot E05 reversal harm at a different observation time and under different information dynamics. |
| C05, F-020/F-021, Q-004 | Extends the conditional value of information from collecting it to retaining it across birth. Acquisition here uses a fixed one-visit rule. |
| C06, F-022–F-024 and H-018 | The high-cost sensing cohort all went extinct. Travel affordability and permanent mortality shape the value of receiving information. Evolved risk avoidance remains open. |
| C07, F-025–F-031, Q-049/Q-050 | C09 inherits the C07 inference kernel. Parent beliefs can improve initial sensor choice under stable mappings. Stale confidence and partial retention alter calibration under reversal, while q=0.50 always remains blank. |
| C08, F-032–F-038 | C08 varied heritable learning and sensing capacity and gave newborns blank memories. C09 fixed that capacity and evolved receptivity to acquired state. Both use occupancy and conditional passive replay; their genotypes, neutral benchmarks and effect sizes differ. |
| H-010, Q-008 | C09 charges a single parent and tests one donor per birth. It motivates separate work on the demographic and informational effects of two-parent reproduction, particularly when environmental regimes or bottlenecks limit mate availability. |

Evidence links and quantitative denominators are in [RESULTS.md](RESULTS.md) and [conditions.csv](results/conditions.csv). No canonical IDs were assigned.
