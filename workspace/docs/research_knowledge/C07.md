# C07 secondary findings and branches

## Learning changed beliefs without changing fixed-policy behaviour

- **Originating experiment and evidence** · C07 implementation `2204783`, all 50 pilot NPZ archives. See [the fixed-policy audit](evidence/C07/fixed_policy_reconciliation.json). Retrospective Codex analysis of experiment records.
- **Original observation** · P2 fixed/frozen and P4 fixed/learning had zero differences in sampled sites, observations, patch actions, rewards, return steps and energy at every matched position. Their learned-memory values differ at 998,400 stored positions and their patch posterior at 449,142, using 10⁻¹² tolerance for beliefs. These are matched array positions, including frozen-after-death positions where applicable.
- **Analytical interpretation** · With equal patch priors and one recorded reading, the probability of the readout's state is `0.5 + (q−0.5) × P(sampled site is reliable)`. For q>0.5 and positive reliability belief, the decision always follows the readout. In the q=0.5 controls the tie draw is shared. Fixed schedules therefore allow calibration changes without changing the action, while adaptive sensing can use the same learned state to select a different observation.
- **Alternative and limitation** · With multiple observations, asymmetric payoffs, confidence-dependent abstention or an informative resource prior, changing confidence may change decisions directly. Current policy equality is structural in the recorded conditions, with matching schedules and reward draws, rather than evidence that memory is generally useless.
- **Classification and links** · Analytical finding supported by raw evidence. Propose a distinct record linked to F-011, Q-002, Q-004, Q-011 and the C03 entropy–accuracy finding. This qualifies the C07 inferred benefit as depending on where the learned belief can affect action. Test the same update under confidence-sensitive actions and multi-reading decisions, preserving P2/P4 as a control for use of knowledge.

## Accuracy above chance can be insufficient to pay for sensing

- **Origin and evidence** · Assistant post-hoc derivation in C07 planning discussion, supplied for verification by the researcher in the recovery handover, independently verified against C07 maintenance, travel, sensing and reward code. C07 pilot condition table and predecessor C04–C06.
- **Derivation** · If (a) is the accuracy of resource choice in the population of trip attempts, its reward probability is (0.85a+0.15(1-a)=0.15+0.70a). Under full-trip accounting, expected reward is (8(0.15+0.70a)), and maintenance plus round-trip travel costs (1+4). With a sensing cost (c) per trip the expected net is (Delta E=5.6a-3.8-c). Break-even accuracy is ((3.8+c)/5.6). At (c=2.5) this is (6.3/5.6=1.125), so even perfect accuracy would have a −0.7-unit expected drift under these assumptions. At (c=0.3125), break-even is 73.4375%.
- **Scope** · This is a post-hoc full-trip calculation, with one maintenance charge and known payoff and cost constants. It applies when the full trip is paid, or as a counterfactual charge to all attempts. Conditioning on observed completed trips under mortality can change the reward probability because reward itself can determine completion. Abortions, abstention, absorbing death and varying costs require path-level accounting. The formula gives no direct survival probability.
- **Associated observation** · In the stable eight-unit, expensive-sensing condition P5 was 72.17% correct conditional on reaching a decision versus P1 at 49.73%. P5 lived 3.555 periods versus P1 at 6.970. At q=0.5 all six approaches made identical resource choices and obtained identical rewards episode by episode, while sensing cost an extra 15 units at 512 energy.
- **Classification and disposition** · Post-hoc analytical finding. Energy ceiling belongs with Q-004/H-007/H-012. The existing C07 accuracy-versus-survival and null-cost candidates provide experimental evidence. Preserve them by reference. Test a learned sensing opt-out and reserve-dependent information budget against no sensing, fixed schedules and a planner with computation costs. Separate changes to action, expected return and survival.

## Stability, reversals and lack of predictive memory

- **Origin and evidence** · C07 `pilot/primary_contrasts.csv`, `condition_means.csv`, `secondary_contrasts.csv` and seed curves. Experimental evidence and retrospective analysis.
- **Observation** · Stable 24-unit adaptive learning improved survival duration over P3 and P4 by 5.73 and 4.97 periods. Stable high-energy late accuracy improved by 11.39 points over P3 with zero mortality. At 512 units in reversal, adaptive learners had 95.31% recovery with a restricted mean delay of 8.03 periods versus 96.50% and 7.88 for fixed learners. At 24 units in unpredictable environments, P5−P3 duration was −0.226, exploratory interval [−1.060,+0.609].
- **Interpretation and alternatives** · Better resource decisions and faster belief recovery are distinct. Fixed exploration may collect more evidence about the alternative site. The recovery criterion, 0.05 hazard and initial prior may also shape the ranking. Independent redraws leave no predictive value in previous mappings, while the oracle knows the current mapping.
- **Classification and disposition** · Preserve the four existing C07 candidates by reference. Link outcomes to H-002/H-004/H-006 and Q-003. Future experiments can separate recoverability from action usefulness by crossing change predictability and sensor usage with matched costs. The reported belief-threshold difference is descriptive.

## Early mortality and access to feedback

- **Origin and evidence** · C07 four-unit boundary in protocol and `pilot/condition_means.csv`. Experimental evidence and Codex interpretation.
- **Observation** · Before the first trip, maintenance leaves three units, and even direct travel needs a reward for alive return. In the stable four-unit setting P5 mean lifetime was 10.1575 periods, with 38.00% maintenance deaths and 48.125% return deaths. P1 averaged 3.46875, with 39.625% maintenance deaths and 59.625% return deaths.
- **Interpretation and alternatives** · Death can truncate access to the feedback required for learning. Early reward luck and energy arithmetic alter which organisms are represented by conditional accuracy. The four-unit results still show a benefit for P5 in this cheap-sensing regime.
- **Classification and disposition** · Exploratory evidence with a separate causal question. Extend the existing early-bottleneck candidate and H-004/Q-001/Q-004. Test matched initial exposure, learning priors and cost perturbations while retaining all deaths and using scheduled-period metrics. No follow-up ran.
