# Project Archean — Evolvable reproductive strategies

**Date:** 27 November 2026  
**Status:** Proposed research branch; not authorised for implementation or execution.  
**Candidate reference:** Evolvable reproduction (separate from the proposed C10 controlled two-parent assay). Permanent identifier to be assigned through knowledge-register review.

## Origin and attribution

The researcher proposed two-parent reproduction to explore more combinations, contrasting inheritance and mutations. The assistant subsequently proposed that reproduction itself could become an evolvable trait, rather than permanently assigning populations to single-parent or two-parent reproduction. The researcher explicitly requested that this extension be retained as an additional research branch, not merely as conversation memory.

## Research question

Under which environmental, energetic and demographic conditions does selection favour single-parent reproduction, two-parent reproduction, or a facultative strategy that switches between them? Can reproductive strategy, genetic recombination and acquired-information transfer coevolve without a direct reward for intelligence or a particular reproductive mode?

## Competing hypotheses

- Single-parent reproduction may favour rapid recovery, reproductive autonomy and persistence under low density or severe scarcity.
- Two-parent reproduction may combine advantageous existing alleles or useful acquired information, but can also disrupt useful combinations or propagate conflicting/outdated beliefs.
- Facultative reproduction may outperform fixed modes when partner availability, ecological conditions or costs change, but its flexibility may carry additional costs.
- Neither mode is assumed superior: neutrality, coexistence, loss of flexibility and extinction are valid outcomes.
- Recombination and mutation are distinct mechanisms; greater recombination does not inherently mean a higher mutation rate.

## Proposed experimental progression

1. First review C09's single-parent information-inheritance results.
2. Establish C10 controlled fixed-mode comparisons: single-parent versus two-parent reproduction, with genetic recombination and acquired-information transfer independently manipulated.
3. In a separate, prospectively specified experiment, introduce heritable variation in reproductive-mode preference or a switching policy. Allow mutation and differential survival and reproduction to determine its prevalence. Distinguish a fixed preference locus from genuinely context-dependent switching; neither is evidence of spontaneous evolution of reproduction itself.
4. Compare fixed single-parent, fixed two-parent and facultative strategies with matched founder distributions, mutation opportunities, energy budgets and offspring investment where feasible. Include a conditional neutral demographic benchmark appropriate to each reproductive mechanism and fresh-seed confirmation of selected findings.
5. Explore energy scarcity, resource abundance, environmental instability, population bottlenecks, mate availability, encounter/search costs, parental contributions, mutation rates, recombination and inherited-information agreement. Separate the effects of these variables before investigating their interactions.

## Evidence and measurements

Preserve full pedigrees with both parent IDs where relevant; inheritance and mutation records; reproductive decisions and failed mating attempts; energy transfers and costs; acquired-state donor identities; reproductive-mode frequencies and switching behaviour; genotype diversity; viable descendant counts; population persistence, extinction and survival. Report individual survival separately from reproductive success and lineage persistence. Treat independent worlds as the statistical units.
