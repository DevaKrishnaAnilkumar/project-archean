# C08 prior evidence review

27 November 2026 · Exploratory evolutionary assay · Baseline 55b7513

The [canonical register](../../docs/RESEARCH_REGISTER.md), [experiment index](../../docs/EXPERIMENT_KNOWLEDGE_INDEX.md), and [branch map](../../docs/RESEARCH_BRANCHES.md) anchor this review.

| Evidence and open records | C08 design, expectation and competing explanations |
|---|---|
| F-001, Q-009 | The early population cap is a source observation. The original v1.1 configuration is missing. Use a birth-blocking cap with no energy ranking, carry over reserves and log energy and demographic events for replay. Apparent enrichment could reflect bottlenecks, mutation or unequal access to birth slots. |
| F-003, F-005 | Navigation reach gains and local cue dependence were measured with supplied policies. Those endpoints do not establish reproductive value. Here choice accuracy is secondary, and balanced founders and passive turnover help distinguish a demographic contrast from a task-level performance effect. |
| F-007, H-009, Q-006 | Copied memory helped in stable conditions and harmed after reversal. Only genotype is inherited in C08, with newborn beliefs reset. No inference about whether transmitting acquired state is useful. |
| F-014, F-025, H-005, H-008 | Prior fixed cohorts link designed learning to scoped survival, without reproduction. C08 allows survival and reproduction to influence trait frequencies without an explicit cognitive reward. Stable cheap sensing is plausibly favourable. Early reproduction, chance reward and founder lineage survival can change the effect. Passive replay tests conditional enrichment under random turnover. |
| F-020, F-024, Q-004, Q-007 | Multi-step information and optional stopping can change energetic value. C08 permits a single affordability-gated visit with inherited propensity, no evolved planning, and a maintenance overhead that also applies to nonsensing learners. Free sensing and null cues distinguish different components of cost. Saved energy may feed reproduction, but that endpoint depends on threshold and cap. |
| F-026, F-030, Q-005 | Confidence, action and recovery can diverge. Here belief can change sensing location; conditional on informative sampling, the one-cue choice generally follows the cue. Report belief and accuracy separately. Learning and sensing are supplied mechanisms. |
| F-027, F-028, H-007, Q-049 | Costly sensing can improve choices while shortening lifetime. Correct the full-trip bound for reward and learning overhead: reward × (0.15 + 0.70a) − 5 − sensing cost − overhead, before birth transfers. This accounting comparison is neither a survival probability nor conditioned survivor energy. Affordability gates can select exposures. |
| F-029, H-006, Q-008 | Independent mapping redraws remove the predictive value of past mappings. Periodic and stable conditions probe different forms of predictability; one reversal frequency cannot establish an optimum. Earlier negative or contrary evidence motivates comparing all regimes regardless of direction. |
| F-031 | At q=0.50 reward and observation cannot update mapping belief. Null cues at positive cost offer an opportunity to see direct sensing expenditure without useful information. Mutation, chance and endogenous population events still produce variation in outcomes. |
| H-018, Q-050 | Maintenance continues when travel is unaffordable, and early action failure can reduce feedback access. Report feedback exposure and competing early mortality explanations with all extinct worlds included. Affordable foraging is required, so the risk-avoidance hypothesis stays open. |

The experiment compares supplied, inheritable capacities and their energetic consequences. Acquired-state inheritance, planning evolution and evolved risk avoidance require separate, approved designs. Complete pedigree and conditional demographic replay allow lineage and neutral-turnover comparisons while keeping population survival as an active-world outcome.
